# Aleatoric and Epistemic Uncertainty Disentanglement for GAN-based Calorimeter Simulations

**Abstract:** Fast simulation solutions based on generative models have emerged as a promising alternative to computationally expensive Monte Carlo simulation for high-energy physics. However, the lack of reliable Uncertainty Quantification (UQ) remains a significant challenge for a safe usage of neural network based models in this context. In this work, we investigate the use of Monte Carlo Dropout (MCD) for uncertainty disentanglement in a Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) trained to simulate electromagnetic calorimeter showers conditioned on the energy of incident particles. To disentangle predictive uncertainty, MCD is applied at inference time to separate uncertainty estimates into its aleatoric and epistemic components. Experimental results show that the estimated Aleatoric Uncertainty (AU) strongly correlates with output variance, while Epistemic Uncertainty (EU) strongly correlates with distance from the training distribution, yielding near-perfect Pearson correlation coefficients and perfect Spearman rank correlations. These findings show that the proposed method can reliably distinguish between inherent data randomness and uncertainty arising from limited model knowledge, enabling principled detection of unrealistic predictions. The proposed approach requires no significant architectural modifications or additional training objectives, making it easy to integrate into existing neural-network based simulation pipelines while remaining computationally efficient compared to ensemble-based alternatives.
